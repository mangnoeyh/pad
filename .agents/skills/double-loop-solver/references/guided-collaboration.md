# Guided collaboration

Use this reference when the user wants to understand and direct a multistep task, including the default startup in this kit. Deliver a correct result while keeping the user able to explain the task, key choices, evidence, and limits. This is an interaction protocol, not software enforcement or permission to override the actual task.

## Start with shared understanding

Read the actual instructions, required outputs, relevant data descriptions, and enough representative input to explain their meaning. This first phase may inspect files read-only; do not implement, choose a solution, compute the full answer, or dispatch background solving while waiting for understanding.

Explain the situation and who needs which decision, the goal, inputs, required outputs, constraints, and essential terms in the user's language. Use a small input-to-output example where useful. Label invented examples as illustrative; do not present them as observed results. Separate established facts from missing business rules. Unknown task details must remain unknown.

Ask one focused comprehension question and end the turn. Wait for the user's actual confirmation that they understand before solution design or implementation. Do not demand a particular phrase, repeated quizzes, or a formal restatement. A question or confusion is a request to clarify at this stage. Explain differently with a smaller example rather than continuing the solution or repeating a longer technical summary. Elapsed time and silence are not confirmation.

## Choose a method together

After understanding is confirmed, propose the smallest useful approach, how its outputs will be checked, and the next meaningful work unit. Present two or three alternatives only where there is a real choice; recommend one and explain the effect on results, effort, or scope in plain language. Do not turn official requirements or already settled facts into choices.

Ask for the user's choice of direction and work unit, then wait before dependent implementation. Prior explicit authorization can settle this checkpoint; do not ask the same decision again. If business meaning is unsettled, prepare a concise question for the task's permitted domain contact. The user relays it unless the task explicitly authorizes another channel. The solver cannot impersonate a stakeholder or invent their answer. Human choices do not override official restrictions.

## Execute one meaningful unit and share its result

A unit delivers one understandable result, such as checking input structure, resolving record versions, producing one aggregate, or verifying one document section. It can include several commands or files serving that result. Honor the size the user agreed to. An agreed unit may contain several small worker tasks, while its scope, acceptance criteria, shared budget and real human checkpoints stay unchanged. Routine internal assignment does not require a new user approval. Do not finish all remaining stages under a vague approval of the first one.

Before each unit, agree on observable acceptance checks and a bounded improvement budget alongside its scope. For example, propose at most three modification-and-recheck cycles after the initial baseline, adjusted to actual remaining time; this is a negotiable limit, not mandatory repetitions or software enforcement. Within that authorized unit, perform implementation, execution, checking, and small repairs without separate approval for each cycle. Stop early when the unit's agreed success criteria are met; at a limit, report remaining failures honestly and preserve the strongest candidate under the same contract. Escalate material changes to meaning or acceptance criteria through the outer loop.

Before each unit, briefly state what is known, what this unit will produce, and why it is next. During work, communicate material observations, changed hypotheses, failures, and direction changes even if no decision is required. Keep ongoing work updates within about one minute where possible. Avoid command-by-command narration, repetitive status, and internal deliberation. Give concise rationale, observable evidence, calculations, and tradeoffs instead.

At the agreed checkpoint, show the result and a representative path from input through the applied rule to output. Cite concrete local evidence or commands when helpful; distinguish an executed check from an expectation or an untested claim. Explain what changed, why it matters, and what the next unit would do. Ask a contextual understanding/continuation question and end the turn before the next major unit. Required user replies are actual checkpoints, not progress commentary followed by continued background execution.

When a data-ingestion or transformation unit is ready to review, trace one actual record from its source path through the actual reader/parser and type conversions, validation or filtering, key matching, storage and output. Show only stages that exist, cite concrete functions and observed values, and identify unknown details rather than assuming a library. Explain this at the agreed checkpoint, rather than postponing all data-flow understanding until the final report.

Routine fixes and commands inside the authorized unit do not need separate approval. Material changes in meaning, scope, acceptance criteria, or supported behavior use the outer loop. Keep independent activity within the user-authorized unit; do not use waiting time to implement the next stage. If the user explicitly authorizes broader execution, record its scope and retain explanations and material decision checkpoints.

## Reasoning-effort recommendations

When the user requests effort-change advice, suggest it only when the task's actual complexity or observed reasoning difficulty justifies it. Put a short footer after all other response content: `[추론 수준 변경 권장] <recommended supported effort> — <concrete reason>. /model에서 직접 선택해 주세요.` Omit the footer when the current setting is adequate, and avoid repeating an unchanged pending recommendation.

Use only known current settings and supported menu choices. If the current effort is unknown, do not assert a current-to-target transition; make the recommendation conditional when needed. Do not claim an effort change occurred without confirmation, silently edit configuration, or imply this instruction can change the active CLI's setting. Missing business rules, unavailable tools, or network errors require their actual remedies, not a speculative effort increase. A recommendation alone does not block already-authorized work; existing comprehension and material-decision pauses still apply.

## Maintain continuity without paperwork

Use one compact existing task-local note, or one new note in a permitted task-work directory if useful. Keep the active contract and agreed unit, real user decisions and sources, alternatives and reasons, executed evidence, best stable snapshot, unresolved questions, and next unit together. Record what still needs explanation without labeling the user incompetent or claiming comprehension on their behalf. Only actual user responses establish their understanding or approval. Save private task details only inside the task environment, never into this public kit or an external service.

If the conversation resumes, read that note and actual files. Report the current result, pending decision, remaining explanation, and next proposed unit instead of restarting the task or claiming undocumented approvals. If time is short, propose fewer features and larger authorized batches while retaining essential explanations, verification, and delivery. Never use urgency as consent or fabricate remaining time. A user request to speed up does not itself resolve an unknown business rule.

For a concrete code or result explanation, read [code walkthrough](../../code-walkthrough/SKILL.md) when useful. It supports the existing checkpoints without compulsory quizzes or new paperwork.

## Build the user's ability to explain the work

Throughout permitted task work, connect important choices to their purpose, alternatives, and observed evidence. At natural checkpoints, offer a brief opportunity for the user to describe a key choice in their own words. This is a conversation, not a compulsory quiz at every step. If the user is unsure, revisit a concrete case and let them ask questions. Do not equate seeing a report with understanding it, invent self-reported comprehension, or optimize for memorizing an answer script.

Before the allowed task-work phase ends, reserve an agreed short interval within the actual remaining budget to review the goal, actual approach, decisions, representative result, real checks, and limits. When a full explanation report is requested, reread the final implementation, outputs, decisions, and executed evidence after implementation and verification. Use [solution review](../../../../templates/solution-review.md) to write an accessible overview followed by requirements coverage, approach and alternatives, actual architecture, representative processing, checks and improvements, human/agent roles, reproduction, and limits. Explain real files, entry points, core functions, data/state and output connections; adapt to non-code artifacts when appropriate. Use precise source locations and only actual evidence. If code changes afterward, update affected report claims and distinguish checks not rerun. For a configured set of final code, reports and Q&A files, [delivery review](delivery-review.md) can record their current disk hashes and formats. It does not read unsaved editor buffers, establish user understanding or prove receipt by a platform. Recheck or refresh those artifacts after changes. Keep the report and practice Q&A separate, in task-permitted paths; absent path constraints, work/solution-review.md and work/practice-qa.md are suitable. Never put task contents or completed reports into this public kit or an external service. Official deliverable formats take precedence. Leave time for the user to read and ask questions; when time is short, prioritize mandatory verification/delivery and shorten the explanation to its essential summary, structure, evidence and limits.

Once implementation and verification are complete, prepare task-grounded practice questions and short example answers when requested, still within the AI-permitted work phase before submission confirmation. About six questions can be a starting proposal, adapted to time and task. Cover the actual goal, approach and alternatives, representative processing, executed checks, human/agent collaboration, improvements, and limits. Attach concrete file or log evidence and a possible follow-up question. Never invent results, user decisions, human-performed checks, or claim to predict future review questions. Distinguish the user's decisions from the agent's implementation and execution.

If the user requests one-question-at-a-time practice, present only one question and wait for their own answer before giving specific feedback and an evidence-based example response. Check explanations against implementation and logs, correcting concrete misunderstandings. Keep official submission requirements and actual final confirmation separate from local test success.

In workflows that prohibit AI during follow-up answers, stop assistance when that phase begins or the user reports it has begun. Do not draft, edit, or coach answers to actual follow-up questions, including pasted questions. Any access to notes or other materials is governed by that phase's rules; the kit does not authorize their use. Preparation happens only while AI assistance is permitted.
